How each model thinks

Every card works the same match: Man City v Fulham, Sat, 21 Nov, odds 1.40 / 5.25 / 7.00. Taking the margin out, the market gives Man City 68.2%. On the log-odds scale that is ln(0.6818 ÷ 0.3182) = 0.762, which is where most of the models start.

The one the app runs.

Logistic regression

Start from the market's log-odds. Each feature pushes up or down by a fixed amount (its weight × how unusual the value is). Add the pushes, then squash back into a chance.

z = logit(m) + b + Σ wj · (xj − μj) ÷ sj    p = 1 ÷ (1 + e−z)
biggest pushes: market log-odds + 0.216, elite × losses + 0.057, league position − 0.038
z = 0.762 − 0.017 + 0.172 (all 12 pushes) = 0.917
p = 1 ÷ (1 + e−0.917) = 71.4% · p × odds = 0.7144 × 1.40 = 1.0002 → back it

Straight lines only: one more loss always counts the same. That is a weakness and also why it doesn't overfit.

Logistic that can bend.

GAM (generalised additive model)

Same as logistic, but each feature gets a curve instead of a straight line: a few "hinges" where the slope can change. So the second loss can count more than the first.

z = logit(m) + b + Σ fj(xj)    f(x) = w0x + Σ wk · max(0, x − knotk)
curves at Man City's values: market log-odds + 0.261, league position + 0.062, opponent's position − 0.053
z = 0.762 − 0.080 + 0.146 = 0.828 → p = 69.6%

Extra flexibility found little real shape: more bets, worse return.

log-loss 0.57168 (beats market)value favourites −1.4% on 2,528your teams −1.1% on 580
Small trees in a row, each fixing the last one's mistakes.

LightGBM (gradient boosting)

Starts at the market's log-odds. Tree 1 looks for groups the market got wrong and adds a small correction; tree 2 corrects what is still wrong, and so on. Here 135 trees of 7 leaves each, each step only 3% of a full correction (the learning rate). "Light" because it buckets features into histograms and grows the leaf with the biggest gain first, so it is very fast.

z = logit(m) + η·tree1(x) + η·tree2(x) + …    each tree fits the gradient y − p
what the 135 trees added (SHAP split): market log-odds + 0.188, goal diff. beyond price + 0.033, opponent's position − 0.016
total from trees = −0.016 (average) + 0.217 = + 0.201
z = 0.762 + 0.201 = 0.964 → p = 72.4%

The closest rival to logistic. It is choosier: half the bets, with the best return. The trees mostly learned the favourite stretch (market log-odds) and recent goal difference.

log-loss 0.57151 (beats market)value favourites +2.4% on 646your teams −4.6% on 155
Many deep trees, averaged.

Random forest

Grows 200 trees, each on a random resample of the matches and a random subset of features, then averages their chances. Each tree is noisy; the average is steadier. Unlike the models above it doesn't start from the market: it gets the market as just one more input.

p = (1 ÷ T) Σ treet(x)
the 200 trees said: 10% of them below 63%, middle 77%, 10% above 87%
average = 75.5% · 78% of trees above the market's 68.2%

Too confident about favourites, and worse than the market on log-loss. With this little signal, deep trees find noise.

log-loss 0.57272 (worse than market)value favourites −0.0% on 3,238your teams +0.4% on 714
Layers of tiny logistic regressions.

Neural network

Inputs (12 features + market log-odds) feed 8 hidden units. Each unit is a weighted sum passed through ReLU = max(0, u), so it switches on only for some patterns. The output is a weighted sum of the units, squashed into a chance.

hk = max(0, Σ Wjk xj + bk)    p = σ(Σ vk hk + c)
units switched on: #2 = 2.60 (× 0.48), #4 = 1.05 (× 0.31), #6 = 0.62 (× 0.46); the other 5 are 0
z = 2.60×0.48 + 1.05×0.31 + 0.62×0.46 − 0.876 = 0.974 → p = 72.6%

Worst on log-loss. It needs far more signal than football prices leave, and it is hard to explain to yourself why it bets.

log-loss 0.57460 (worse than market)value favourites −1.6% on 3,078your teams +0.2% on 711
Does a club beat its price, or was it luck?

Bayesian shrinkage

For each club: how often it won beyond what the market said, r = mean(y − m). Then shrink r towards 0 by how much is likely noise. τ² is how much clubs truly differ; it is estimated from the data as the spread we see minus the spread luck alone would make.

θ̂ = r × n ÷ (n + σ² ÷ τ²)    p = m + θ̂
Man City: r = + 0.0218 over 384 matches
σ² = 0.199, τ² ≈ 1e-6 → shrink = 384 ÷ (384 + 1,99,284) = 1.9e-3
θ̂ ≈ 0 → p = market = 68.2%

The honest finding: once you account for luck, no club has a lasting edge over its price. Villa's +6.9 points of over-performance is luck-sized. So a club's name alone is no reason to back it.

The verdict. Logistic (0.57137) beats the market's 0.57225 by the most, then LightGBM (0.57151) and GAM. Only logistic and LightGBM make money on value favourites: +1.3% and +2.4%. The flexible models (forest, network) are worse than the market itself: there isn't enough signal, so they learn noise. Bayesian says no club has a lasting edge. On your teams alone, both good models were slightly negative: the market prices big clubs well. The model's edge comes mostly from other clubs. A natural next step: average logistic and LightGBM, and test whether the blend beats each one.
